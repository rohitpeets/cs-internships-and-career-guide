# LinkedIn Setup

LinkedIn is where recruiters and professionals find each other. It matters less than your classes and projects, so spend about an hour setting it up, then about 15 minutes a week keeping it alive.

## Profile checklist

- [ ] **Photo.** Face in good light, plain background, shoulders up. A phone photo is fine.
- [ ] **Name.** The same name as on your resume. If people often mispronounce it, add a short note in About, or use LinkedIn's name pronunciation recording if your app shows it.
- [ ] **Location.** Your real location (the city or area where you live or go to school). Recruiters filter by it. If you want to work elsewhere, say so in About.
- [ ] **Custom URL.** Change it to `linkedin.com/in/yourname` in settings.

## Headline

The line under your name. If you leave it blank, LinkedIn shows only your school or "Student".

**Before:** `Student at Example State University`

**After:** `CS student at Example State | Python, Java, SQL | Built a study-planner web app`

Pattern:

> [Year] [Major] at [School] | [2-3 skills you've used] | [one thing you built]

Add "Seeking Summer 2027 internship" once you're applying.

## Example profile

This is a made-up sophomore. Copy the shape, not the words.

**Headline:** CS student at Example State | Python, Java, SQL | Built a study-planner web app

**About:**

> I'm a sophomore studying computer science at Example State. I like building small tools that fix everyday problems. Last semester I built a study planner that 12 classmates use. Before college I worked in my family's restaurant, where I learned to stay calm during a rush. I'm looking for a software internship for summer 2027. Email: name@email.com

Four short sentences: who you are, what you build, one non-tech strength, what you want, and your email.

**Project entry:**

> **Study Planner** (Python, Flask, SQLite) — Built a web app that turns a class syllabus into a weekly schedule, used by 12 classmates, by writing a date parser and a calendar view. Code and demo: github.com/yourname/study-planner

**Non-tech job entry:**

> **Line Cook, Family Restaurant** — Kept orders on time for up to 80 customers a night, by prepping the station ahead and calling out delays early. Trained 2 new hires.

Write bullets the same way as your resume: what you did, how you know it worked, and how you did it. See [resume: bullet formula](resume.md#bullet-formula-xyz). Never invent numbers. If you don't have one, describe the work in plain words. Keep your LinkedIn and resume consistent.

## Fill in the rest

- **Education:** school, major, expected graduation date.
- **Experience:** any job counts, even non-tech. One or two bullets each.
- **Projects:** each with a one-line description and the GitHub or live link.
- **Skills:** 5 to 10 you have really used. Ask a professor or classmate to endorse one or two.
- **Featured:** pin your best project link or a live demo near the top. This is the easiest place to show your work.
- **Clubs and leadership:** ACM, other clubs, events you organized.
- **Languages:** list them if you speak more than one.

## Find alumni

Alumni are your best contacts. Steps:

1. Search for your school and open its LinkedIn page.
2. Click **Alumni** (or **People**).
3. Filter by where they live, where they work, and what they studied. Try "software", "engineer", or a company you like.
4. Open 5 profiles. Pick people who graduated 3 to 8 years ago.
5. Send a note. LinkedIn limits connection notes to about 200 characters, and free accounts can be limited in how many notes they can send each month, so keep it short. If the note box is gone, send the request and message them once they accept.

Template that fits (under 200 characters once you fill it in):

> Hi [Name], I'm a [Year] CS student at [School]. I saw you went here too. Could I ask you one quick question about your work at [Company]?

Aim for about 5 requests a week. When someone replies, ask your one question. If they're friendly, ask for 15 minutes. After any chat, follow up the next day with thanks. If they don't answer, send **one** nudge a week later ("Just checking in. No worries if you're busy.") and then stop. Full scripts, including a follow-up, are in [networking](networking.md#alumni).

After you apply to a company, it's fine to message an alum there with one line saying you applied and why. See [applying: referrals](applying.md#referrals).

## Job search settings

- **Open to Work.** In your profile, choose Open to work and set the roles, places, and start date you want. Pick **recruiters only** (LinkedIn says only people using its Recruiter product can see it) so you don't get the green frame on your photo. Some people like the frame, but recruiters-only is the safer first choice.
- **Job alerts.** Search "software engineer intern" with your location and save it as an alert. Add "new grad" later. Make one alert per kind of role.

## Keep it alive

- Once a month, add anything new: a project, a club role, a class you finished.
- Check your messages once a week. Reply to recruiters politely, even to say no.
- Update your headline when your goal changes (internship, then new grad).

## Posting (optional)

You don't have to post. If you want to, write about one thing you learned: "I fixed a bug where my app crashed on empty input. Here's what I learned." Short, specific, and true is enough. Comments on other people's posts also count.
