# Pick Your Track

"CS internship" does not only mean software engineering. This guide covers seven paths, what each one asks for, and how to start. You do not have to choose now. Read the one or two that sound interesting, try a 4-week starter project, and see if you like it.

The job board has a page for each track. They are sorted by job title, so a few postings may land in the wrong place.

**Quick picker**
- You like building apps and websites: [Software](#software).
- You like code that runs on small devices, like sensors and robots: [Embedded and firmware](#embedded-and-firmware).
- You like how chips and circuits work: [Hardware and chips](#hardware-and-chips).
- You like breaking things to learn how they work: [Security](#security).
- You like fixing computers and networks: [IT and networking](#it-and-networking).
- You like finding patterns in numbers: [Data and ML](#data-and-ml).
- You want a mission-driven job, or a clearance: [Government and defense](#government-and-defense).

**Words used here**
- **CTF:** "capture the flag." A security puzzle contest. You find hidden text (a "flag") by solving challenges.
- **Certification (cert):** a paid exam from a company that proves you know a topic.
- **Co-op:** a paid, full-time job with a school-approved employer, often for a whole semester. See the [glossary](README.md#glossary).

## Software

**The work.** You build and fix apps, websites, and services that run on servers. It is the largest track, so it has the most openings and the most applicants.

**Classes that help.** Data Structures, Algorithms, Databases, Software Engineering.

**4-week starter project.** Build a small web app that stores data, such as a study-group scheduler. Week 1: pick 3 features. Weeks 2 and 3: one feature per week. Week 4: put it online and get 3 people to try it. More in [projects](projects.md).

**Certs.** Skip them. Projects and experience matter more.

**Contests and clubs.** ACM chapter, hackathons, ICPC.

**Job titles to search.** Software Engineer Intern, Software Developer Intern, Web Developer Intern, Backend Intern, Full Stack Intern.

**How interviews differ.** Coding problems on a screen, then a project talk. See [interviews](interviews.md).

Job list: [software track](../track-software.md).

## Embedded and firmware

**The work.** You write code for small computers inside devices: cars, medical tools, drones, appliances. The code is usually C or C++. You work close to the hardware, so you read data sheets and use tools like an oscilloscope.

**Classes that help.** Computer Organization, Operating Systems, Digital Logic, any C or C++ course, Microcontrollers if offered.

**4-week starter project.** Buy a cheap microcontroller board (Arduino or Raspberry Pi Pico, about $10 to $25). Week 1: blink an LED. Week 2: read a sensor. Week 3: show the reading on a screen or send it to your laptop. Week 4: pack it into one small device, like a temperature logger, and film a 1-minute demo.

**Certs.** Skip them. A working board on your desk beats a cert.

**Contests and clubs.** Robotics club, your school's IEEE student branch, rocketry or drone clubs.

**Job titles to search.** Embedded Software Intern, Firmware Intern, Embedded Systems Intern, Controls Intern, Test Automation Intern.

**How interviews differ.** Expect C questions: pointers, memory, bits. Be ready to explain a hardware project in detail. Many companies still ask a few standard coding problems too.

Job list: [embedded track](../track-embedded.md).

## Hardware and chips

**The work.** You help design and test chips and circuit boards. Digital design means describing circuits in a hardware language like Verilog. Verification means proving the design works. Others work on board layout or lab testing.

**Classes that help.** Digital Logic, Computer Architecture, Circuits, an HDL (hardware description language) course.

**4-week starter project.** Free practice on [HDLBits](https://hdlbits.01xz.net/wiki/Main_Page) in Verilog. Week 1: gates and simple logic. Week 2: adders. Week 3: counters and flip-flops. Week 4: a small state machine, such as a traffic-light controller, with a testbench (code that checks your design).

**Certs.** Skip them.

**Contests and clubs.** IEEE student branch, robotics club, FPGA or chip-design clubs.

**Job titles to search.** Hardware Engineering Intern, Digital Design Intern, Design Verification Intern, ASIC Intern, FPGA Intern, Electrical Engineering Intern.

**How interviews differ.** Mostly circuit and logic questions at a whiteboard, plus a deep talk about a class or lab project. Fewer LeetCode-style problems.

**Timing.** Many large employers post in late summer and fall. Check each company's student page, and ask your engineering career office about co-op programs.

Job list: [hardware track](../track-hardware.md).

## Security

**The work.** You find weak spots before attackers do, defend networks, or watch for attacks. Entry-level paths: security analyst (watching alerts), application security (reviewing code), and penetration testing (ethical hacking, which is hard to land first).

**Classes that help.** Networking, Operating Systems, Intro to Security, Linux.

**4-week starter project.** Week 1: learn the Linux command line on [OverTheWire](https://overthewire.org/wargames/). Week 2 and 3: solve 10 beginner challenges on [picoCTF](https://picoctf.org) (free). Week 4: write up two of them step by step in a public repo. Writeups show how you think.

**Certs.** CompTIA Security+ is the common first one and shows up in many government and contractor job posts. It costs a few hundred dollars, so pay for it only if you are aiming at those jobs. Skip it if you are mainly applying for software roles.

**Contests and clubs.** [National Cyber League](https://www.nationalcyberleague.org) (online, individual or team; the fall season has a fee and runs through the fall), the Collegiate Cyber Defense Competition ([CCDC](https://www.ccdc.io); schools field a team, so ask your department), CTF clubs.

**Job titles to search.** Security Intern, Cybersecurity Analyst Intern, SOC Analyst Intern, Application Security Intern, Information Security Intern.

**How interviews differ.** Scenario questions: "You see odd traffic on a server. What do you check first?" Networking basics, Linux, and one CTF or lab you can talk about.

**Money.** CyberCorps Scholarship for Service pays tuition and a stipend for cybersecurity students, in exchange for government work after. Your school must take part. See [government](government.md#programs-to-know).

Job list: [security track](../track-security.md).

## IT and networking

**The work.** You keep an organization's computers, accounts, and networks running. Every company has an IT team, so these jobs exist in every city. It is a good way to get paid experience early, and many people move into cloud, security, or software from here.

**Classes that help.** Networking, Operating Systems, Databases, Systems Administration.

**4-week starter project.** Build a home lab. Week 1: install Linux in a virtual machine. Week 2: set up a file server and two user accounts. Week 3: set up a small network in a free simulator such as Cisco Packet Tracer. Week 4: write a 1-page runbook (steps for fixing common problems).

**Certs.** More useful here than in other tracks. CompTIA A+ (basics) and Network+ help for help-desk jobs. CCNA (Cisco's networking cert, about $300, good for 3 years) helps for network roles. Take one only after you have seen the topic in class or in your lab, and ask your department if it offers discounts.

**Contests and clubs.** Cyber defense teams, IT or networking clubs, campus IT help desk (a paid student job).

**Job titles to search.** IT Intern, Help Desk Technician, Systems Administrator Intern, Network Engineer Intern, Cloud Support Intern, Technical Support Intern.

**How interviews differ.** Troubleshooting questions ("a user can't connect, what do you do?") and customer-service skills matter as much as coding.

Job list: [IT track](../track-it.md).

## Data and ML

**The work.** You clean data, find patterns, build charts, and sometimes train models. Titles run from data analyst (spreadsheets, SQL, charts) to machine learning engineer (code and models). Analyst roles are easier to land first.

**Classes that help.** Statistics, Databases, Linear Algebra, Data Structures, any Python data course.

**4-week starter project.** Pick a public data set about something you care about. Week 1: load it in Python and clean it. Week 2: answer 3 questions with SQL or pandas. Week 3: make 3 clear charts. Week 4: write a 1-page summary of what you found, and what you are not sure about.

**Certs.** Skip them at first. A project with a clear write-up counts more.

**Contests and clubs.** Data science club, ACM, campus research groups, hackathons with data themes.

**Job titles to search.** Data Analyst Intern, Data Science Intern, Machine Learning Intern, Business Intelligence Intern, Data Engineering Intern.

**How interviews differ.** SQL questions, basic statistics, and "walk me through a data project." Machine learning roles add model questions. See [interviews](interviews.md).

Job list: [data track](../track-data.md).

## Government and defense

**The work.** Federal agencies, national labs, and defense contractors hire for software, cyber, embedded, data, and engineering. Many jobs need U.S. citizenship, and some need a security clearance.

**Classes that help.** The same as the track you want, such as Networking for cyber or Digital Logic for hardware.

**4-week starter project.** Week 1: make a [USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov) account and save a search. Week 2: read 5 student postings and write down what they ask for. Week 3: check your resume against them. Week 4: apply to one program, or ask your career office which employers recruit on campus.

**Certs.** Security+ can help for cyber jobs. Skip it otherwise.

**Contests and clubs.** National Cyber League, CCDC, IEEE student branch, [IEEEXtreme](https://ieeextreme.org) (a 24-hour team programming contest; it needs IEEE student membership).

**Job titles to search.** Pathways Intern, Student Trainee, Cyber Intern, Software Engineer Intern with "defense," "aerospace," or "national lab" in the company name.

**How interviews differ.** Often a panel or a phone interview with a manager, plus questions about teamwork and following rules. Honest answers on forms matter a lot. See [government](government.md).

**Timing.** These open in the fall, not spring. See [when to apply](government.md#when-to-apply).

Job list: [government track](../track-gov.md).

## Co-ops and timing

Co-op programs vary by school, so ask your career office early. A co-op may take a full semester, so you plan your classes around it. Postings for many large employers appear in late summer and fall for the next year, but local employers, labs, and government programs follow their own calendars. Check each employer's page, and save the dates in your tracker ([applying](applying.md)).
