# 4-Year Timeline

A map, not a rule. If you're behind, start where you are today. If you're taking a heavy course load or working a job, do less. That's fine.

**The big date to know:** summer internships at big companies open about **July to September of the year before**, and many fill up by October. "Summer 2028 internship" means applying in fall 2027.

## Freshman year

**Fall**
- [ ] Go to class, do your own work, go to office hours.
- [ ] Join your ACM chapter or CS club.
- [ ] Early programs for first-years mostly close in the fall. Apply to one or two if you like, but they're long shots ([networking](networking.md)).

**Spring**
- [ ] Write your first [resume](resume.md).
- [ ] Build one small [project](projects.md), ideally with a friend.
- [ ] Apply to summer research programs (REUs) and campus jobs.

**Summer**
- Any job is a good summer. If you have spare time, build a project or take a free course.

## Sophomore year

**Fall**
- [ ] Take Data Structures seriously. It's the class interviews are built on.
- [ ] Apply to sophomore programs and internships (see [applying](applying.md)).
- [ ] Go to the career fair. Talk to local employers too.
- [ ] Volunteer to run one thing in a club.

**Spring**
- [ ] Keep applying. Smaller and local companies hire into spring.
- [ ] Ask a professor about research.
- [ ] Build a bigger project with a team.

**Summer** (before junior year)
- [ ] Coding practice: 4 or 5 problems a week for 8 to 10 weeks before fall.
- [ ] Polish your resume and apply the day postings open.

## Junior year

- **Fall:** Apply in the first two weeks of postings (July to September). This internship often leads to a full-time offer.
- **Spring:** Interviews and offers. If nothing comes through, plan a summer of research, a serious project, or a local job. That still counts.

## Senior year

- **Fall:** Apply for full-time jobs (they open in late summer), or accept a return offer. Thinking about grad school? Ask professors for letters early in the fall.
- **Spring:** Decide, finish your degree, and thank the people who helped. Then help the students behind you.

## Coding interview practice

Most internship applications include an online coding test or interview.

- Start after you've taken Data Structures (or while taking it).
- Learn the patterns: arrays, strings, hash maps, two pointers, recursion, trees, graphs.
- Use one language you know well (Java, Python, or C++).
- Follow [NeetCode](https://neetcode.io)'s list of 150 problems and practice them on [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com).
- Time yourself, and practice explaining out loud as you code.
- [ICPC](https://icpc.global) practice and contests count as practice too.

## Note for F-1 students

Rules depend on your situation, so check everything with your DSO (Designated School Official, the person at your school who handles F-1 rules).

- **On-campus jobs** are allowed with hour limits during the semester. Ask your DSO first.
- **CPT** (Curricular Practical Training) lets you work off campus in your field while you study. It usually needs one full academic year of study first and is often tied to a course or credit. Your DSO must approve it, for that specific employer and dates, **before** you start. Never start early.
- **OPT** (Optional Practical Training) is usually how you work after graduation (up to 12 months, plus a 24-month STEM extension with eligible employers). Ask your DSO about it before your last year.
- **Read postings carefully.** Skip ones that say "no CPT/OPT" (the job board marks these 🚫) or "US citizens only" (🇺🇸). "We don't sponsor visas" is not the same thing: some of those companies still hire interns on CPT. Ask the recruiter.
- **Application forms** ask "Are you authorized to work in the US?" and "Will you need sponsorship now or in the future?" Answer truthfully every time, and ask your DSO how to answer for your case.
- Apply widely. Smaller companies, universities, and research labs are often more open to international students.
