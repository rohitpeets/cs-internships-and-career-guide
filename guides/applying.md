# Applying and Interviews

For most students, the hard part isn't being good enough. It's applying early, applying enough, and getting a person to look at your resume.

## Timing

- Big-company summer internships open about **July to September of the year before** and often fill by October. Apply in the first week or two after a posting opens.
- Smaller and local companies, government, and research labs hire later, into spring.
- This job board lists new postings daily with the date each company posted them, so check it often in the fall.

## Volume

Expect to send a lot of applications. 50 to 100 in a season is normal, and most will get no answer. That's how it works for almost everyone, not a sign that you're bad.

Track everything in a simple spreadsheet: company, role, date applied, link, referral (yes/no), status. Follow up once if you hear nothing after 2 to 3 weeks and you have a contact there.

## Referrals

A referral means someone at the company submits your name. It's the best way to get a human to read your resume.

- **Who:** alumni from your school, upperclassmen who interned there, people you met at fairs or events.
- **How:** have a real conversation first. Then ask: "I'm applying to [role, link]. Would you be comfortable referring me? Here's my resume." Make it easy for them.
- If they say no or don't answer, thank them and move on.

## Online assessments (OAs)

Many companies send a timed coding test ([HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com), [CodeSignal](https://codesignal.com), or similar) right after you apply, often before a person looks at your resume.

- 60 to 90 minutes, 2 to 4 problems.
- Practice timed problems ([coding practice](timeline.md#coding-interview-practice)).
- Do it in a quiet place, in a language you know well.
- Don't cheat or use AI tools. Companies check, and they will ask about your code later.

## Interviews

**Recruiter call (15 to 30 minutes):** why this company, what you've built, when you graduate, work authorization. Have a 30-second answer to "tell me about yourself."

**Technical interview:** solve a problem while talking through your thinking. Ask questions first, start with a simple solution, then improve it. Test your code with an example.

**Behavioral interview:** questions like "tell me about a time you worked on a team." Answer with **STAR**: the Situation, your Task, the Action you took, and the Result. Prepare 5 stories from projects, jobs, and clubs: a team conflict, a mistake you fixed, something you led, something hard you learned, a deadline you hit.

Practice out loud with a friend, or at your career center's mock interviews.

## Rejections and silence

You will get many. Every person who got an internship also got rejections. After each season, look at where you got stuck:

- **No responses at all:** fix your resume, get referrals, apply earlier.
- **Failing OAs:** more timed practice.
- **Failing interviews:** more mock interviews, better STAR stories.

## Offers

- You usually get 1 to 3 weeks to decide. It's fine to ask for more time politely.
- Once you accept an offer, don't back out for another one. It burns bridges.
