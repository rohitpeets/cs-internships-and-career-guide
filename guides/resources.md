# Resources

Too many lists make it hard to start. Begin with the top picks, then use the groups that fit you.

*Spot-checked November 18, 2026: the community, experience, and conference pages below were opened that day, and eligibility is what each page said. Prices, dates, and rules change, so confirm before you rely on anything.*

## Top picks by need

**Start learning**
- [CS50x](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/): a free intro to CS, a good start if you have never coded.
- [The Missing Semester](https://github.com/missing-semester/missing-semester): the terminal, Git, and tools classes skip.
- [OSSU Computer Science](https://github.com/ossu/computer-science): a free full CS curriculum. It's huge, so use it as a map, not a to-do list.

**Build**
- [GitHub Skills](https://skills.github.com): free hands-on Git lessons.
- [Project-Based Learning](https://github.com/practical-tutorials/project-based-learning): tutorials by language, including Java.

**Get experience**
- [Forage](https://www.theforage.com): free, self-paced job simulations.
- [Parker Dewey](https://www.parkerdewey.com): short paid micro-internships for students and recent grads; no student fee listed.
- [Extern](https://www.extern.com): remote projects. It advertises a free trial, then paid plans; check the price first.

**Find your people**
- [ACM](https://www.acm.org): student chapters and contests. Join the one on your campus.
- [CodePath](https://www.codepath.org): courses in AI, web, mobile, cybersecurity, and interview prep, free for CS students per its site. Spring 2027 courses fill on a rolling basis.

**Practice interviews**
- [NeetCode](https://neetcode.io), [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org), and peer mocks on [Pramp](https://www.pramp.com) (its FAQ says free, but reports say sessions moved platforms, so check). Plans and Java references: [interviews](interviews.md#resources).
- [Nyrion](https://nyrion.ai): practice for coding rounds where you work with an AI assistant. Free plan; Pro is $19 a month.

**More job lists**
- [Summer 2027 internships](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/Summer2027-Internships) and [new-grad positions](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions): community-kept Simplify lists, updated daily in November 2026.
- [Handshake](https://joinhandshake.com) (through your school), and [USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov) for federal jobs.

**Money**
- **SMART** ([smartscholarship.org](https://www.smartscholarship.org)): a scholarship-for-service for STEM students. The site says full tuition, stipends, and employment with the Department of War after graduation. It doesn't state eligibility or service terms in the text I could read, so check before you plan around it.
- **CyberAI Scholarship for Service** (SFS; formerly CyberCorps, and the [SFS site](https://www.sfs.opm.gov) still uses the old name): tuition and a stipend for cybersecurity students in return for government work. NSF's call lists U.S. citizens or permanent residents, and your school must take part. Risk: in 2025 some scholars reported rescinded federal offers, and scholars who can't find federal work in a set period can owe money back. Check current terms.
- Ask financial aid, your department, and student government about travel grants and emergency funds.

## By situation

### Data and ML
- [Kaggle Learn](https://www.kaggle.com/learn): free short courses on Python, pandas, and more.
- [Hugging Face Learn](https://huggingface.co/learn): free machine learning courses.
- [Data Science for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/Data-Science-For-Beginners): a free 10-week course.
- [Data Engineering Zoomcamp](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/data-engineering-zoomcamp): free, 9 weeks; a certificate needs a live cohort.
- [Break Through Tech](https://breakthroughtech.org): AI and data programs plus paid short "Sprinternships." Its FAQ: open to undergraduates at U.S. colleges, all majors, free for fellows. Deadlines vary.

### Computer engineering and hardware
- [Nand2Tetris](https://www.nand2tetris.org): build a computer from logic gates up.
- [HDLBits](https://hdlbits.01xz.net/wiki/Main_Page): Verilog practice, one small circuit at a time.
- [Embedded Engineering Roadmap](https://github.com/m3y54m/Embedded-Engineering-Roadmap): a long, optional learning map.
- An IEEE student branch or robotics club. More in [tracks](tracks.md#embedded-and-firmware).

### Cybersecurity
- [picoCTF](https://picoctf.org): free beginner security challenges. It's now run as CyLab Security Academy; older log-ins still work, per its homepage.
- [OverTheWire](https://overthewire.org/wargames/): Linux and security games.
- [National Cyber League](https://www.nationalcyberleague.org): online competition for students. Fall 2026 registration cost $45 and closed in October, so watch for the next season.
- [Awesome CTF](https://github.com/apsdehal/awesome-ctf): CTF tools and practice sites; not updated since mid-2024, so some links may be dead.
- [CyberSeek](https://www.cyberseek.org): a map of cybersecurity jobs. More in [tracks](tracks.md#security).

### IT and home labs
- [awesome-sysadmin](https://github.com/awesome-foss/awesome-sysadmin) (moved to this address) and [awesome-selfhosted](https://github.com/awesome-selfhosted/awesome-selfhosted): free tools and apps for a home lab.

### First-generation students
- Ask whether your campus has a first-generation student office or a federal TRIO program. TRIO serves low-income and first-generation students and students with disabilities. You can't apply to the government directly: you must be eligible and accepted by a funded program that serves your school or area, and funding changes, so ask. The [Department of Education TRIO page](https://www2.ed.gov/about/offices/list/ope/trio/index.html) is an older page (last updated 2021), so confirm details with your school.
- Your career center, and the referral steps in [applying](applying.md#referrals).

### Women and underrepresented groups
Many offer mentors, events, and recruiter connections. None of the pages below listed a student price, so check cost and eligibility before you join.
- [ColorStack](https://www.colorstack.org): Black and Latinx CS students. Slack community, workshops, partner career fairs. Membership rules are on its member-application page.
- [NSBE](https://www.nsbe.org) and [SHPE](https://www.shpe.org): Black and Hispanic engineers and STEM students. NSBE says student membership is open to all students; SHPE says it's open to everyone. Both run national conventions.
- [SWE](https://swe.org): women in engineering and tech; says membership and events are open to all, with a collegiate membership.
- [AnitaB.org](https://anitab.org): runs the Grace Hopper Celebration; says its work centers on tech workers who face barriers to entry.
- [Rewriting the Code](https://rewritingthecode.org), [Out in Tech](https://outintech.com), [oSTEM](https://ostem.org), and [AISES](https://www.aises.org): women in tech, LGBTQ+ people in tech, and Indigenous students in STEM.
- [SEO Tech Developer](https://tech.seo-usa.org): free and virtual; per its site, a $6,000 stipend for a six-week paid summer residency. It asks for a sophomore at a four-year U.S. school (or a two-year student transferring) with some programming experience. Its latest posted window was January to March 2026, so watch for next year's dates.
- Code2040 closed in 2026. Skip old links to it.

### Wellbeing
Job searching is stressful. Rejections are normal and say little about you.
- Many campus counseling centers offer free sessions. Ask at the student health office.
- [988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org): call or text 988, any time, free and private. It's for any kind of emotional distress, not only emergencies. If you're in danger now, call 911.

### Funding conference trips
- [Grace Hopper Celebration](https://ghc.anitab.org) (2026 is in Anaheim; its page listed no dates yet) and [Tapia](https://tapiaconference.cmd-it.org) (for students, faculty, and professionals in computing; "Tapia 2027" details were still coming): neither page mentioned student travel support, so email the organizers and check their lists.
- Ask your department, ACM chapter, and student government for travel money, and say how you'll share what you learn. Many conferences also have volunteer or student rates.
