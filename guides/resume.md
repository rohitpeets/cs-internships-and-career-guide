# Your First Resume

A resume is a one-page summary of what you've done. Recruiters spend a few seconds on it, so keep it clean and easy to scan. You don't need work experience to make a good one.

## The rules

- **One page.** Always, until you have many years of work.
- **Plain layout.** One column, one font, black text. No photo, icons, skill bars, or tables.
- **Save as PDF.** Name it `FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf`. Copy the text out of the PDF and paste it into a plain text file: if it comes out jumbled, the software that reads resumes will see it jumbled too.
- **Professional email.** Your name, not a nickname.

## Sections, in order

1. **Header:** name, email, phone, city and state, LinkedIn, GitHub.
2. **Education:** school, major, expected graduation, GPA if 3.0 or higher (many applications ask for it anyway), Dean's List or honors, relevant classes.
3. **Projects:** your best 2 or 3. This is the main section when you have no experience.
4. **Experience:** any job counts, including retail, food service, campus jobs, or a family business. It shows you show up.
5. **Leadership and activities:** club roles, competitions (ICPC, hackathons), tutoring, volunteering, events you organized.
6. **Skills:** languages, tools, and frameworks you have really used.

## Bullet formula

Each bullet is: **action word + what you did + how or what came of it.**

- Weak: "Worked on a website."
- Better: "Built a study-planner web app in React and Node.js; 12 classmates used it during finals."
- Better, without a number: "Built a study-planner web app in React and Node.js with login, reminders, and 20 unit tests."
- Weak: "Helped at club events."
- Better: "Organized 6 club meetups for 40+ members; handled booking, signup, and setup."

Good numbers: real users, tests, response time, records handled, people led. **Never invent a number.** Interviewers ask about them. If you can't measure it, describe what it does and how you built or tested it. Lines of code is not a good number.

Start bullets with words like Built, Designed, Wrote, Tested, Organized, Led, Fixed, Reduced. Use 2 to 4 bullets per item.

## No experience? Use this

- **Projects:** class projects (if allowed), personal apps, hackathon builds, team projects.
- **Coursework:** 4 to 6 relevant classes (for example, Data Structures).
- **Clubs and competitions:** members and officers both count.
- **Skills from other jobs:** teamwork, customer service, and handling money are real skills.

Only write true things. Interviewers will ask about every line.

## Tailor it

Read the job posting. If it asks for skills you really have (say, Java and SQL), make sure those exact words appear, and move your most relevant project to the top.

## Tools

- **Google Docs or Word** with a plain one-column template is all you need.
- **Optional:** Overleaf with the "Jake's Resume" template, a free LaTeX template many CS students use. Only if you're curious about LaTeX.

## Common mistakes

- Typos. Read it out loud, then ask a friend.
- Two pages, or long paragraphs instead of bullets.
- Skills you can't talk about in an interview.
- Missing links to GitHub or projects.

**Get it reviewed** by an upperclassman who has had an internship, and by your career center.
