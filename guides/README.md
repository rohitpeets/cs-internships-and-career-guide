# Student Guides

Short, practical guides for computer science and computer engineering students at any U.S. school. They're written for students who have never had an internship and don't know where to start, including late starters, transfers, and students who work.

**You will not do all of this, and you don't need to.** Your classes come first. Pick one or two things a semester.

Some of your classmates coded in high school or already have an internship. That's a head start, not a final score. Steady work beats it: one project a semester, a few applications a week during hiring season, and showing up to the same club and office hours until people know you.

**Working or commuting? Try the 5-hour week.** About 1 hour on class help (office hours or tutoring), 2 hours on one project, 1 hour on applications or your resume, and 1 hour on people (a club meeting, or one message to an upperclassman or alumnus). It's slower, and it still works. Keep it steady and don't try to catch up in one weekend.

## How internships work

- An **internship** is a short paid job, usually 10-12 weeks in the summer. A **co-op** is longer, often a full semester of full-time work. A **new-grad role** is your first full-time job after you graduate.
- Big companies hire summer interns almost a year ahead (fall for the next summer). Smaller and local companies often hire in spring. Government and defense roles often open early in the fall because of background checks.
- Most freshmen don't get an internship. That's normal. A campus job, research, or a project is a good first summer.
- Aim for **any** internship by the end of sophomore year, even a small or local one. Then climb: local company, then a mid-size one, then a big one if you want. Each one makes the next easier.
- Skim the [job board](../README.md) once a month, even before you apply. Note the skills and languages that keep showing up, and learn one of them.

## Year 1 checklist

| Step | Done looks like |
|---|---|
| Go to class and office hours | A professor or TA knows your name, and you've asked at least three questions in office hours. |
| Join a CS club ([networking](networking.md)) | You've been to three meetings and know two upperclassmen well enough to ask a question. |
| Build one small [project](projects.md) | A repo a stranger can run by following the README, plus a one-line result ("Used by 12 classmates to split rent"). |
| Put it on [GitHub](github.md) | Your profile has your real name, and the project is pinned with a clear README. |
| Write a [resume](resume.md) | One page, saved as a PDF, reviewed by at least one person (career center, upperclassman, or professor). |
| Start your [wins file](#start-a-wins-file) | One doc with every project, job, and result so far. |
| First summer | Any job counts, and it goes on your resume. Research or a finished project is great. |

## Year 2 checklist

| Step | Done looks like |
|---|---|
| Data Structures | You can explain and code a hash map lookup, a stack, and a breadth-first search without notes. |
| Bigger team [project](projects.md) | Two or more people committed code, it runs or is live, and you can name one number (users, speed, data size). |
| Coding practice ([plan](timeline.md#coding-interview-practice)) | 2-3 problems a week, every week, from one list. |
| [Apply](applying.md) | Applications tracked in a spreadsheet, including local and mid-size companies, plus one career fair where you talked to five employers. |
| [LinkedIn](linkedin.md) | Profile matches your resume, with a photo and your project links. |
| Research or campus job | You asked one professor about research, or you hold a campus job you can describe in one bullet. |
| Second summer | An internship of any size, or research, or a job plus one finished project. |

## Start a wins file

Make one document today and keep adding to it. List every project, job, club role, and result, with real numbers: users, hours saved, grade, contest rank, dollars raised, people served. Add to it the week something happens, while you still remember the details.

> Oct 2026. Club treasurer. Tracked a $1,800 budget in a shared spreadsheet; event came in $90 under budget.

Your resume, LinkedIn, and interview stories all come from this file, so you never start from a blank page.

## Glossary

- **Internship:** a short job (usually summer) for students, most often paid in tech.
- **Co-op:** a longer work term, often a full semester of full-time work, sometimes repeated.
- **New grad:** a full-time job meant for people graduating soon or just graduated.
- **OA (online assessment):** a timed coding test you take online, often the first step after you apply.
- **Referral:** someone at the company submits your name or vouches for you, so a recruiter is more likely to look.
- **ATS (applicant tracking system):** the software companies use to collect and sort applications. A person still usually makes the decision.
- **REU (Research Experiences for Undergraduates):** paid summer research programs at universities, funded by the National Science Foundation.
- **Return offer:** an offer to come back (for another internship or full-time) after your internship ends.
- **Handshake:** a job site many colleges use, where employers post jobs for students at your school.
- **Career fair:** an event where employers set up tables and talk to students. Bring your resume.
- **Tech screen:** a first technical interview, often 45-60 minutes of coding with an engineer.
- **Behavioral interview:** questions about how you've handled real situations ("Tell me about a time you...").
- **Clearance:** U.S. government approval to work with classified information. The employer starts it, it can take months, and it usually requires U.S. citizenship.
